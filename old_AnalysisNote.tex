\begin{abstract}
\centering
\begin{abstract}
\centering
Precise timing measurements in the CMS electromagnetic calorimeter (ECAL) provide powerful discrimination against pileup and enhance sensitivity to physics signatures involving nonprompt and delayed particles.
However, the default Run~II ECAL time reconstruction, based on the ratio method, is susceptible to degradation from out-of-time pileup.
In this work, a cross-correlation (CC) timing algorithm is introduced that leverages the existing pileup-aware ECAL energy reconstruction to improve time determination at the crystal and object levels.

The performance of the CC algorithm is evaluated using proton--proton collision data and simulation, employing refined timing resolution measurements, generator-level time estimates, and offline calibrations.
Relative to the Run~II default method, the CC algorithm improves the single-crystal time resolution by approximately 30~ps.
At the object level, electron and photon time resolutions of order 100~ps are achieved, compared to typical Run~II values of approximately 300~ps.
These results demonstrate that substantially improved ECAL timing performance is achievable with the current detector, with direct relevance for Run~III data taking and future precision timing applications.
\end{abstract}
